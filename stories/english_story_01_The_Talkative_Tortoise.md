# The Talkative Tortoise

Tilly the tortoise lived in a sunny meadow with her friends: the wise Owl, the busy Squirrel, and the gentle Rabbit. Tilly loved her friends, but she loved one thing more—her own voice. She talked all day long.

"Good morning, Owl! Did you see the dewdrops? I counted thirty-seven on one leaf. Well, maybe thirty-eight. One was very tiny. Do you think dewdrops have dreams? I dreamt I was a cloud once. It was very floaty..."

Owl would nod politely, but his eyelids often grew heavy.

One day, the animals decided to have a picnic by the sparkling pond. Everyone brought something to share. Rabbit brought clover buns, Squirrel brought acorn pie, and Owl brought a jar of honey. Tilly brought herself, and she brought plenty of stories.

As they set the blanket, Tilly began to talk.

"Did I ever tell you about the time I raced a turtle? Well, it was actually a snail. He was very slow. Or was it me who was slow? Let me think..."

Squirrel put out the plates. Rabbit arranged the buns. Owl uncorked the honey. But Tilly just kept talking.

"And then," she continued, "the wind blew the flower petals all over. They were pink, but also a bit purple. Purple is an interesting color, isn't it? My cousin's shell has a purple speck. Just one. Do you think one speck is enough to be called purple? I think two specks..."

Her friends tried to listen, but their ears were tired. They were hungry too. The delicious smells from the picnic made their tummies rumble.

Finally, Squirrel couldn't wait anymore. He reached for a clover bun.

Tilly gasped. "Squirrel! I'm not finished with my story! It's not polite to eat when someone is talking."

Squirrel pulled his paw back. The picnic was not fun anymore. Rabbit looked at the ground. Owl stared at the clouds. Tilly was so busy talking, she hadn't noticed how quiet her friends were.

Just then, a loud *WHOOOOSH* swept through the meadow. A gust of wind grabbed the picnic blanket! It flipped it over, scattering buns, pie, and the honey jar high into the air!

"Oh no!" cried Rabbit.

The food landed with a *splish-splash* right in the middle of the pond. The honey jar floated away on the water, and the buns and pie sank to the bottom.

The four friends stood at the edge of the pond, very sad. The picnic was ruined, and nobody had gotten to eat a single bite.

Tilly looked at her sad friends. For the first time all day, her mouth was closed. A quiet thought formed in her mind: *They couldn't enjoy the picnic because they couldn't get a word in... or a bite in.*

She took a deep breath. "I'm sorry," she said softly. "I was so busy talking that I forgot to listen. And I forgot to let you eat. A picnic isn't just about talking. It's about sharing."

Owl blinked his big, kind eyes. "We love hearing your stories, Tilly," he hooted gently. "But sharing means everyone gets a turn to speak. And to eat."

Tilly nodded. "I understand now. Maybe I can make up for the lost picnic by doing something special?"

The next day, Tilly invited her friends back to the meadow. This time, she was quiet at first. She had spent the morning thinking of a plan.

"I have an idea," she announced. "Let's build a new picnic table, right here under this big oak tree. Then, the wind can't take our blanket away!"

Tilly didn't talk while they worked. She listened to Rabbit's ideas, and Squirrel's, and Owl's. They found smooth stones for legs and a wide, flat board for the top.

When they were done, they had a wonderful, sturdy table. They sat down, and Tilly passed the food around first.

"Now," she said with a smile, "I will tell you a new story. It's about a tortoise who learned how to listen. But first, does anyone want more clover buns?"

Her friends laughed. They shared the food, and then they shared stories, each animal taking a turn. Tilly talked, but she also listened. And she found that the stories from her friends were just as wonderful as her own.

**Moral of the story: Talking is wonderful, but listening is a gift. When we share our voices and our time, everyone feels happy and heard.**
