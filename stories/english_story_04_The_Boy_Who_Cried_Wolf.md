# The Boy Who Cried Wolf

Once upon a time, in a sunny green valley, there lived a young shepherd boy named Eli. His job was to watch over the fluffy white sheep on the hillside every day.

It was a very important job. The sheep were soft and gentle, and they trusted Eli to keep them safe. Most days, Eli sat on his favorite rock, counting the sheep and watching the clouds drift by.

But some days, Eli felt bored. There was no one to talk to, only the sheep who would say, "Baa!"

One day, Eli had a mischievous idea. He stood up and shouted as loud as he could, his voice echoing across the valley.

"Wolf! A big gray wolf is chasing the sheep! Help!"

Down in the village, the farmers heard the shout. They dropped their tools and ran up the hill as fast as they could. Eli saw them coming and had to hide his giggle.

When the farmers arrived, panting and worried, they looked everywhere. "Where is the wolf, Eli?" asked Old Man Miller.

Eli laughed. "There is no wolf! I was just pretending!"

The farmers were not happy. They had to walk all the way back to their work. "That was not a funny joke, Eli," they said.

The next week, Eli was bored again. The sheep were just chewing grass, and the sun was too warm.

"Wolf! Help!" he cried out again. "A wolf is here!"

The farmers heard him and ran up the hill once more. They looked behind bushes and under trees. But of course, there was no wolf.

"You tricked us again!" said the baker, wiping her brow. "This is not kind."

Eli just smiled. He liked making people run.

Then, one evening, as the sun began to set, Eli saw a shadow move in the trees. A pair of yellow eyes glowed in the twilight. A real wolf, large and quiet, stepped out from the forest.

Eli’s heart began to beat very fast. He jumped to his feet and shouted, "Wolf! A real wolf! Help! Please help!"

His voice was scared this time. It cracked with fear.

Down in the village, the farmers heard him. But they shook their heads. "Oh, Eli is playing his trick again," they said.

"He has fooled us twice. He won’t fool us a third time."

They did not come. The wolf stepped closer to the sheep. Eli was so scared. He tried to scare the wolf away himself, throwing sticks and shouting, but the wolf just growled.

In that moment, Eli felt very sorry. He was sorry he had not told the truth. He was sorry he had made the farmers not believe him.

Then, just as the wolf was about to step into the field, old Mr. Finn from the far end of the valley heard the real fear in Eli’s cries. He came running with his big dog and a lantern.

The wolf saw the light and the dog, and it turned and ran back into the dark forest. The sheep were safe.

Eli ran down the hill, tears on his cheeks. He found the farmers, who had heard Mr. Finn and now came slowly up the hill.

"I am so sorry," Eli whispered. "I lied, and then when I needed help, you did not come. I understand why."

The farmers looked at the scared boy and the safe sheep. Old Man Miller put a hand on Eli’s shoulder. "It is good you are safe, Eli. But words are very important. When you lie, you break trust. And trust is like a glass vase—once broken, it is hard to put back together."

Eli nodded. He promised to never cry wolf again unless it was real.

And from that day on, Eli was an honest boy. And whenever he felt bored on the hill, he would just sing songs to the sheep, who would listen and say, "Baa!"

And everyone believed him when he spoke.

***

**The Moral of the Story:** When you tell lies, people will not believe you, even when you are telling the truth. It is always best to be honest, so people know they can trust your words.
