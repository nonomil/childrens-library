# Jack and the Beanstalk

Once upon a time, in a little blue house on a green hill, lived a boy named Jack. Jack lived with his kind mother and a cow named Daisy.

One sunny morning, Daisy gave no milk at all.

"Oh dear," said Jack's mother. "We must sell Daisy at the market. Please take her today."

Jack sighed. He loved Daisy. But he knew they needed the money for food.

On the path to the market, an old man with a twinkly smile stopped them. "I'll give you five magic beans for your cow!" he said.

"Magic beans?" Jack's eyes went wide.

"They will bring you a great surprise," the man chuckled.

Jack traded Daisy for the beans and ran home, very excited.

"Oh Jack!" his mother said. "We needed food, not silly beans!" She tossed them out the window and sent Jack to bed with no supper.

The next morning, Jack woke up to a strange sight. Where the beans were thrown, a GIANT BEANSTALK had grown! It was tall, tall, TALL, reaching right up into the clouds.

"Well," said Jack, "I must see where this goes."

He climbed up, up, up the beanstalk. He went past birds' nests and fluffy clouds until he reached a land in the sky.

There was a huge castle! Jack tiptoed inside. In the kitchen, he saw a giant woman making porridge.

"Please, may I have some breakfast?" asked Jack politely.

The giant woman smiled and gave him a bowl. But then—BOOM! BOOM! BOOM!—her giant husband came home.

"I smell a little boy!" the giant roared. "Fee-fi-fo-fum!"

The giant woman hid Jack under a pot. The giant had a sack with a golden hen. He made the hen lay a golden egg, then fell fast asleep, snoring like thunder.

Jack saw his chance! He quietly took the golden hen and tiptoed back to the beanstalk.

Down, down, down he climbed, home to his mother. The hen laid golden eggs! Now they could buy all the food they needed.

A few days later, Jack climbed the beanstalk again. This time, he found a magical harp that played beautiful music all by itself.

"Let me sing you a lullaby," the harp sang sweetly.

The giant woke up again! "Fee-fi-fo-fum!" he bellowed.

Jack grabbed the singing harp and ran. The giant chased him! Jack scrambled down the beanstalk as fast as he could. He shouted, "Mother! Bring the axe!"

WHACK-WHACK-WHACK! Jack’s mother chopped the beanstalk. The giant tumbled down and landed—SPLAT—in a big patch of soft mud. He was surprised, but not hurt.

"You stole my treasures!" the giant grumbled, covered in mud.

"You stole our cow!" Jack said bravely. "It wasn't fair."

The giant thought about this. "Perhaps... I was hungry, and I didn't think," he said slowly.

Jack’s mother gave the giant some hot porridge. The giant smiled. "Thank you."

From that day on, the giant was kind. Sometimes, he even sent down golden eggs from the clouds, just to share.

**And what did Jack learn?**

Jack learned that being brave can lead to big adventures. But he also learned that sometimes, the best treasures aren't gold or singing harps. The best treasures are kindness, sharing, and doing the right thing—even when it's hard.

**The End.**
